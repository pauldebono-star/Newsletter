Montenegro & International Protection

1. EU Accession & Negotiation Process

State of play as of September 2026: Montenegro has provisionally closed 18 of 33 negotiating chapters, most recently Chapters 8 (Competition Policy) and 29 (Customs Union) in July 2026. The remaining 15 chapters are at 90–100% technical readiness. The EU and Montenegro are working to an accelerated timeline: all remaining chapters closed by end-2026, a first draft Accession Treaty by end of September 2026, and EU membership targeted for 2028. Chapters 23 (Judiciary & Fundamental Rights) and 24 (Justice, Freedom & Security) — which cover asylum, migration and border management — remain the hardest, requiring structural reform rather than administrative compliance.
  • Lead institutions: European Commission (DG NEAR), Council of the EU, Montenegro's Ministry of European Affairs
  • Key milestone to watch: closure of Chapters 23 & 24

2. National Legislation – Asylum & International Protection

Montenegro's core asylum framework is the Law on International and Temporary Protection of Foreigners (in force since 2018). The procedure runs in two stages: an "intention" declaration giving immediate legal effect, followed by a formal application within 15 days that opens substantive examination. Montenegro is party to the 1951 Refugee Convention, 1967 Protocol, and the 1954/1961 statelessness conventions. Most recently, an amended Law on Foreigners entered into force in early 2026, digitalizing procedures and aligning more closely with EU standards. Temporary Protection for Ukrainian nationals (in place since March 2022) has been extended for existing beneficiaries until 4 March 2027.
  • Key body: Ministry of Interior (asylum office)
  • Watch for: further legislative amendments tied to closing Chapter 24

3. Migration & Asylum Trends – Border Management & Statistics

Montenegro sits on the Western Balkan migration route, historically a transit corridor rather than a destination. Regional arrivals have fallen sharply — from roughly 192,000 in 2022 to about 35,000 in 2025 — helped by tighter regional cooperation and border controls. The main nationalities detected on this route are Syrian, Afghan and Turkish, with the Croatia–Bosnia border the principal exit point from the region.
  • Data sources: Frontex Risk Analysis (quarterly detections), IOM Displacement Tracking Matrix (DTM, quarterly flow-monitoring surveys), UNHCR regional updates
  • Trend to watch: whether the multi-year decline in transit numbers continues through 2026

4. Government & Political Updates

Border management sits under Montenegro's Ministry of Interior. A flagship project is a turnkey electronic surveillance system for the "blue border" along Lake Skadar, providing 24/7 monitoring and rapid-response capability as part of Chapter 24 commitments — work began March 2026 with a 15-month build. The EU has granted an additional €3.9 million for integrated border management. Domestically, EU accession remains the government's central policy narrative, with Prime Minister Spajić repeatedly setting near-term deadlines for chapter closures.
  • Key ministry: Ministry of Interior (border & asylum policy)
  • Key EU partner: European Border and Coast Guard Agency (Frontex) operational agreement

5. Workshops & Trainings (UNHCR, IOM, Frontex, JCP, ICMPD)

Montenegro's border and asylum officials take part in ongoing regional capacity-building activity delivered by UNHCR, IOM, Frontex, the Joint Coordination Platform (JCP) and ICMPD. Frontex runs recurring regional trainings across the Western Balkans and Turkey — document fraud detection (including FIELDS workshops for document experts), nationality screening, interviewing techniques, and firearms detection — having trained roughly 250 experts from the region to date. ICMPD has maintained a Western Balkans presence for over 30 years, supporting policy dialogue and capacity-building. IOM and UNHCR contribute training on protection-sensitive migration management and flow monitoring as part of the same regional cooperation framework.
  • Recurring formats: FIELDS workshops (document experts), joint Frontex/IOM/UNHCR regional trainings, ICMPD policy dialogues
  • Organisations to track for Montenegro-specific sessions: UNHCR, IOM, Frontex, JCP, ICMPD
