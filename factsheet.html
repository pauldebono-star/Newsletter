Montenegro & International Protection

1. EU Accession & Negotiation Process

Montenegro opened EU accession negotiations in 2012 and is the current front-runner among candidate countries, targeting membership by 2028. Of 33 negotiating chapters, 18 are provisionally closed as of mid-2026, with the rest at 90–100% technical readiness. The government aims to close all remaining chapters by end-2026 and have a first draft Accession Treaty ready by end of September 2026. Chapters 23 (Judiciary & Fundamental Rights) and 24 (Justice, Freedom & Security) — which cover asylum, migration and border management — are the most demanding, requiring structural reform rather than administrative box-ticking.
  • Lead institutions: European Commission (DG NEAR), Council of the EU, Montenegro's Ministry of European Affairs
  • Key milestone to watch: closure of Chapters 23 & 24

2. National Legislation – Asylum & International Protection

Montenegro's core asylum framework is the Law on International and Temporary Protection of Foreigners (in force since 2018, amended since). The procedure runs in two stages: an "intention" declaration giving immediate legal effect, followed by a formal application within 15 days that opens substantive examination. Montenegro acceded to the 1951 Refugee Convention and 1967 Protocol in 2006, and is party to the 1954 and 1961 statelessness conventions. A broader Law on Foreigners was amended in early 2026 to digitalize procedures and align more closely with EU standards. Ukrainian nationals have held Temporary Protection status since March 2022, most recently extended for beneficiaries until 4 March 2027.
  • Key bodies: Ministry of Interior (asylum office), UNHCR Montenegro (monitoring & support)
  • Watch for: further EU-alignment amendments as part of Chapter 24 closure

3. Migration & Asylum Trends – Border Management & Statistics

Montenegro sits on the Western Balkan migration route, historically a transit corridor rather than a destination. Regional arrivals have fallen sharply — from roughly 192,000 in 2022 to about 35,000 in 2025 — helped by tighter regional cooperation and border controls. The main nationalities detected on this route are Syrian, Afghan and Turkish, with the Croatia–Bosnia border the principal exit point from the region. IOM runs regular flow-monitoring surveys across Montenegro, Albania, Bosnia, North Macedonia, Serbia and Kosovo; Frontex publishes quarterly detection statistics for the same route.
  • Data sources: Frontex Risk Analysis, IOM Displacement Tracking Matrix (DTM), UNHCR regional updates
  • Regional cooperation: a Steering Committee (EU Commission, Frontex, EUAA, IOM, UNHCR, all six Western Balkan partners) sets joint priorities through 2030

4. Government & Political Updates

Border management sits under Montenegro's Ministry of Interior, with EU financial and technical support flowing through Integrated Border Management grants (an additional €3.9 million granted in 2026). A flagship project is a turnkey electronic surveillance system for the "blue border" along Lake Skadar, providing 24/7 monitoring and rapid-response capability, part of Chapter 24 commitments, begun March 2026 with a 15-month build. Domestically, EU accession remains the government's central policy narrative, with Prime Minister Spajić repeatedly setting near-term deadlines for chapter closures.
  • Key ministry: Ministry of Interior (border & asylum policy)
  • Key EU partner: European Border and Coast Guard Agency (Frontex) operational agreement

5. Workshops & Trainings (UNHCR, IOM, Frontex, JCP, ICMPD)

Montenegro's border and asylum officials take part in an ongoing regional capacity-building programme run jointly by Frontex, EUAA (formerly EASO), IOM and UNHCR under the EU-funded "Regional Support to Protection-Sensitive Migration Management in the Western Balkans and Turkey" (IPA II) initiative. Training areas include combatting trafficking in human beings, detection of falsified documents, nationality screening, interviewing techniques, and firearms detection. ICMPD has supported the region for over 30 years through policy dialogue and capacity-building. Frontex alone has trained roughly 250 experts from the Western Balkans and Turkey since the programme began.
  • Recurring formats: FIELDS workshops (document experts), joint Frontex/IOM/UNHCR regional trainings
  • Coordinating bodies: Frontex, EUAA, IOM, UNHCR, ICMPD, Joint Coordination Platform (JCP)
